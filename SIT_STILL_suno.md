# SIT STILL — Suno package

Original song. Part 1 is a sunny, laid-back indie-pop groove at 92 BPM. A fake ending snaps into a
fast speed-metal build at exactly double tempo (184 BPM) that never comes back down.

---

## HOW THE TRANSITION WORKS

- **The fakeout is a false ending.** Act one thins out layer by layer and looks like a fade-out. The
  listener relaxes. One full second of silence. Then one slammed chord and the drums are already at
  full speed. No build-in, no fill, no warning.
- **Exact double tempo.** 92 → 184 BPM. The handclap pattern on 2 and 4 becomes the snare on every
  beat. The downbeat grid never shifts, so the cut feels violent but not sloppy.
- **Same words, reversed.** "Sit still" becomes "Don't sit still." "Nothing's on fire" becomes "Now
  it's on fire." The lyric motifs are inherited, so the metal half is the same song, not a new one.
- **The hook melody becomes the guitar lead.** The falsetto "sit still" line is what the twin guitars
  play in the solo at double speed. That is the DNA carry-over.
- **Always building.** Act two climbs a half step at the pre-chorus and again at the bridge, adds a
  layer per section, and never has a drop back. It ends on a hard stop, not a fade.

## SUNO TIPS FOR THE HARD FLIP

1. Use the newest model (v4.5 or later). Older models tend to smear genre changes into a blend.
2. If one generation still softens the cut, generate act one alone (indie style prompt, lyrics up to
   the silence break), then use **Extend** from the end of the outro with the speed-metal style
   prompt and act two lyrics. Two style prompts stapled with Extend is the most reliable way to get
   a truly abrupt change.
3. Keep "no fade out" in the style prompt. Suno loves to fade, and a fade kills the "always building"
   ending.

## Novel Idea: Tempo-Locked Fakeout

Most genre-flip songs change tempo loosely and let the drums re-establish the grid. This one locks
the second half at exactly 2x the first, so the ear registers the metal section as the same pulse
pulled tight rather than a new track spliced on. The handclap becomes the snare, the falsetto hook
becomes the guitar lead, and the lazy "sit still" chord loop is the same four chords played as
galloping eighth notes. If Suno honors it, the cut sounds like the song itself snapping, not a
playlist skipping. To push it further, try adding "second half is the first half at double speed"
to the style prompt.
