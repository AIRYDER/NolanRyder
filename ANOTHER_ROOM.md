# ANOTHER ROOM

A psychedelic synth-pop party song that turns into cinematic hybrid-bass the moment someone opens a door. Two people at a house party spend the whole night making sure they're never alone together. Every room is a different genre. The same hook is sung bright in D major and then dark in D minor.

- **The MGMT half** is the living room and the kitchen: 100 bpm, fizzy synths, a deadpan duet, and falsetto stacks.
- **The Kai Wachi half** is the basement and the roof: 150 bpm half-time, growling midtempo bass, strings, and a low dark-pop vocal.

---

## Style / Production Prompt, one-shot (856 chars, fits the 1000-char limit)

```
Psychedelic synth-pop fused with cinematic hybrid-bass dubstep. Part one: 100 bpm, bright D major, fizzy plasticine analog synths, kiting arpeggio hook, cushy drum machine, rubbery funk bassline, handclaps, deadpan conversational male and female leads trading lines, stacked falsetto harmonies, slightly robotic vocal doubles, tape wobble, giddy and wry. Muffled bass thuds through a wall before every change. Drops: 150 bpm half-time, the same synth hook replayed in D minor, growling midtempo bass with precise sidechain, trap-influenced snare, orchestral strings, dark atmospheres, low chest dark-pop vocal with hip-hop phrasing, chopped vocal hooks, brutal yet beautiful. Final drop: lifted back to bright major, melodic future-bass supersaw chords, huge reverb snare, soaring male falsetto. Bright surface, dark undertow. Ends on a front door closing.
```

**Exclude styles:** `pop-punk, metal, screaming, lo-fi, country`

**Sliders, if your Suno version has them:** Weirdness about 60%, Style Influence about 70%.

Artist names are left out of the prompt on purpose, since Suno doesn't take them. The sounds are described instead.

## Song DNA

- **Danger.** A crowd is the only place two people can still be near each other without having to talk.
- **What physically happens.** Two people who live together arrive at a stranger's party three minutes apart and move room to room, each avoiding being alone with the other. They keep ending up in the same room anyway. The last door is the roof, and then their coats.
- **Config.** True dual lead, 50/50, and no stock casting. Both are deadpan in the party. In the basement she takes the low chest voice. On the roof he takes the soaring falsetto.
- **Core motif.** One five-note synth hook, scale degrees 1-3-5-6-5. In D major it is D-F#-A-B-A. In the drops the third and sixth are lowered: D-F-A-Bb-A. The tail of the minor version (A-Bb-A, then back to D) is the album's lullaby cell: scale degrees 5, b6, 5, 1. That link is optional, but it ties this song to the record for free.
- **Tempo logic.** 100 to 150 is 3:2. The triplet eighths of the party groove become the straight eighths of the drop grid, so the bounce of part one turns into the hi-hat roll of part two. At 150 the half-time pulse is 75.
- **Question to statement.** The bright choruses ask "Is there another room?" and rise. The basement answers "There's always another room" in minor. On the roof the question comes back, gets a flat answer, and ends on "There isn't another room."
- **Ambiguity.** Do they leave together? The ending is a door closing and two sets of footsteps, and it doesn't say.

**Anti-corniness check (concept level).** The lyrics are all scenes: shoes, a cat, a plant, forty-one magnets, a speaker. The thesis is never stated. The plainest line is the last one ("Third landing"), which only works because the bridge set it up. The most "lyric-like" line is "I'd love to see the rest of the house", and it's an excuse a real person would make. Importance signals on the final drop are limited to supersaw chords, falsetto and a reverb snare.

## Novel Idea: genre changes are foreshadowed through the wall

Every genre change is heard before it arrives. Before each door opens, the next section's bass is already thudding through the wall, muffled. The door opening is a low-pass filter snapping open in one beat. Each style change then has a physical cause, because someone opened a door, and the listener has already heard what's behind it.

If Suno ignores the muffled bleed, it can be added after the fact. Copy the first eight bars of Drop 1, low-pass it around 300 Hz, set it about 20 dB down under Pre-Chorus 2, and open the filter across the last beat before the door. I can script that.

## Novel Idea: one hook, three moods

The hook is the same melody in major (party), minor (basement) and major again (roof). The two big hits are the same tune with its brightness taken out and then put back, so the drop is made from the song's own DNA.

## Novel Idea: the chorus is a question until the roof

The chorus is a rising question. The basement turns it into a statement. The roof gives the question its answer. The shape of the sentence follows the story: asking, resigning, and finally running out of rooms.
