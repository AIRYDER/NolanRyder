# HOLD STILL

Warm late-night indie-pop groove that gets its power cut mid-word, then never stops
accelerating. One tempo grid, three perceived speeds, no comedown.

---

## Suno — Style / Production Prompt (fits the 1000-char limit)

Warm hazy late-night indie pop with a disco-funk groove, 176 BPM felt as a lazy half-time 88, G major. Smooth breathy male lead lifting into falsetto, clean plucky guitar, round funky bass, hand claps, soft synth pads; a dry close female voice joins late, a third above on the second chorus. The song fakes its ending: a sleepy fading outro is killed mid-word by a power-cut tape stop, two seconds of silence, one spoken female line. Then an ABRUPT distorted bass impact and a relentless build: running four-on-the-floor kick, accelerating snare rolls, rising screaming synth lead, stuttered vocal chops. It explodes into fast aggressive drumstep at full 176 in E minor: machine-gun distorted bass hammering the chorus rhythm, huge screaming leads, gang-shouted chant, glitch stutters, female shouted commands; then the drums double into full two-step drum and bass. Energy never plateaus, every eight bars adds and nothing leaves. No breakdown, no comedown, no outro: ends on a hard cut at peak.

# Blueprint

## Danger sentence

Asking someone to hold still is the last calm thing you'll say all night, and the
person you said it to is the reason it ends.

## Song identity

- **Config:** TRUE DUAL LEAD by handover, ~55/45. Lead B (male) owns the whole
  pop half melodically and never raises his voice. Lead A (female) gets four dry
  lines and a harmony in the pop half, then one spoken line in the dark, then she
  fronts the entire velocity half. He survives the second half only as a vocal
  chop inside the bass, until one plain breathless line before the cut.
- **States:** State A (warm live-band pop, sunset variant) for the first 2:11.
  Then State D (velocity / escape) using State E's bass vocabulary
  (machine-gun bass, transient violence) for the rest. Nothing in between.
- **BPM:** 176 for the entire file. The pop half is played as a lazy half-time
  feel (perceived 88). See Novel Idea 1.
- **Key:** G major → E minor at the power cut. Same key signature, no key change,
  the pivot chord (Em) is already the first chord of the chorus.
- **Drop type:** DnB / velocity, staircase-shaped (four tiers, never a plateau).
- **Sanctioned weird thing (ONE):** the fakeout is diegetic. The pop song is
  killed mid-word by the party's power being cut, and the chorus predicted it
  ("Nobody's cut the lights yet. Stay 'til somebody does."). The transition is a
  plot event, not a production trick.
- **The brief, honoured structurally:** after the cut, energy is monotonic. Every
  eight bars adds something and nothing is ever removed. There is no second
  breakdown, no final chorus, no outro. The song ends on the loudest bar it has,
  mid-word.

## Story / emotional arc

What physically happens, in order:

1. 1:50 a.m., upstairs room at a house party. The good music is downstairs,
   coming through the floor. He's on the arm of a couch by an open window.
2. She has had her coat on since eleven and has not left. He has noticed. A cup
   on the sill is sweating a ring into somebody's book.
3. He offers the other half of the couch. He promises not to make it a thing.
4. She sits. Knee on knee. A car alarm three streets over. Ice goes soft in a cup
   that isn't his. He says he could do this until sunrise; she doesn't have to.
5. She tells him she saw him notice the coat. He is, in fact, making it a thing.
   They sing the hook together once.
6. The song drifts toward sleep. Mid-word, somebody cuts the lights. Silence. A
   laugh in the dark, a door.
7. In the dark, she says: get up, the last train is at ten to three.
8. Back stairs, not the front. No coat. Over a fence. He loses a shoe. She says
   keep going. Cut.

Arc: WARM → WARMER → ASLEEP → DARK → SPRINT. Emotional intensity climbs the whole
way; energy dips to zero for two seconds and then climbs the whole way too. The
only place energy exceeds intensity is Drop 1 (9 vs 8), which is the moment the
song pays out rather than withholds.

## Core motif + harmonic identity

**CORE MOTIF — "hold still", two notes, scale degrees 5 → 3 (D → B in G), rhythm
short-LONG.** Stated twice, the second time a step lower (C → A), giving a
four-note sighing figure: D–B, C–A. The intro guitar plays it as the riff before
anyone sings it.

Why it survives the genre flip: 5 and 3 of G are b7 and 5 of E. Over the drop's
E-minor bass the same two pitches are still consonant, so the screaming lead can
play the exact chorus notes and land them on E. The listener hears the hook
speeding up, not a new song.

**HARMONY**
- Verses: **G – Bm7 – C – Cm** (I – iii7 – IV – iv). The borrowed iv is the sun
  going down; it's the one melancholy chord in the warm half.
- Pre-chorus: **Am7 – C – D – D/F#**, bass walking F# → G into the hook.
- Chorus: **Em – C – G – D** (vi – IV – I – V). Warm, wide, unashamed. The motif
  sits on 5 and 3 so it is stable over every chord: 9 and 7 over C, root and 6
  over D.
- False outro: Em – C, low-passed, slowing, cut on the "sti—".
- Build and all four drop tiers: **E pedal.** Bass on E hammering the motif's
  short-LONG rhythm. Every 8 bars the last bar walks E – F# – G – (E). No
  modulation. The build is done with rhythm, register and density, not key.

## Transition design

**1. Chorus 2 → False Outro → Power cut (the fakeout).**
The false outro must be *convincing*: claps stop, the guitar fades, the plate
opens to maximum, a low-pass closes slowly, and he sings each "hold still" softer
and later than the last. It should sound exactly like a warm song ending. Then,
on the third syllable, an instant power-down tape stop (pitch dives to nothing in
under 200 ms), then **true silence** for about two seconds. No reverb tail. Room
tone only, a laugh somewhere in the room, a door. Her line is spoken with no
music and no reverb. If the listener isn't half-sure the file has ended, the
transition has failed.

**2. Silence → Build (the abrupt badass one).**
The first sound after her line is a single distorted bass impact on the downbeat
at 176, the loudest single transient in the song. Immediately after it the kick
starts four-on-the-floor at 176 with a high-pass that opens from 300 Hz to full
across 16 bars. Snare rolls go quarters → eighths → sixteenths → 32nds, four bars
each. The lead synth plays the motif's interval and climbs a step every four
bars: D–B, E–C, F#–D, G–E. His voice is chopped, then stuttered, then shredded
into pitch-rising grains. Her lines are clipped, rhythmic, and sit in the gaps.
One beat of silence at the very end, with his "hold—" cut off, then the drop.

**3. Drop 2 → Drop 3 (the second gear shift).**
Drumstep (snare on 3) flips to full two-step DnB on the downbeat with no fill.
Everything else stays. This is the third perceived tempo of the song on the same
grid, and it's where the chant changes its one word.

**4. Drop 4 → End (the cut).**
Mid-word, on "HOLD—". Absolute silence. No fade, no reverb tail, no outro.

## Drop design (the staircase)

**Emotional purpose:** velocity. Two people running for the last train. It is
not a fight and not a machine; it is a sprint, and the aggression is the
aggression of not being allowed to stop.

**What every tier inherits (named):**
- The **machine-gun bass** plays only the "hold STILL" short-LONG rhythm on E
  and never any other pattern: da-DAAA, da-DAAA, da-da-da-da-DAAA.
- The **screaming lead** plays the chorus notes D → B and lands on E.
- The **gang chant** is the hook itself, shouted.
- The **intro guitar riff** comes back in Drop 4, distorted.

**Bass / vocal call-and-response (arrangement, not EQ):** the bass hammers under
the chant and goes *silent* under her commands. Her lines and the bass alternate,
never overlap. The gaps are where the drop breathes.

**Sub placement:** sub only on the landing syllable of each chant ("STILL", "ON").
Absent everywhere else so the landing is felt in the chest.

**Drums:** Tiers 1–2 drumstep (kick 1, snare 3, breaks filling the gaps). Tiers 3–4
full two-step. Fills every 8 bars in Tier 1, every 4 in Tier 2, every 2 in Tier 4.

**Silence:** one beat before Drop 1. None inside the drop. The brief is "always
building", so the negative space all lives *before* the drop and the cut supplies
the only rest afterward.

**The one-word turn.** Tiers 1–2 chant HOLD STILL, his ask shouted back at him by
a crowd while everything moves. Tier 3 changes one word: HOLD ON. Same two notes,
same rhythm. It is said, not explained. "Hold on" means both *wait* and *don't let
go*, and the song refuses to pick.

## Automation / evolution

- **Pop half:** plate size and stereo width grow slowly across 2:11. Low-pass
  closes through the false outro. Then everything to zero.
- **Build:** kick high-pass 300 Hz → open. Noise riser. Snare-roll density
  doubles every four bars. Lead pitch climbs a step every four bars. Chop grain
  size shrinks.
- **Drop tiers:** layer count only ever increases. Tier 2 adds bass layer 2 and
  the octave lead. Tier 3 doubles the drum pattern and starts chopping her voice.
  Tier 4 adds the distorted guitar riff and fills every two bars.
- **Loudness:** the second half must be measurably louder than the first. This
  is the payoff record, not the withholding one.

## Mix priorities

Arrangement first, per the spec.

- **Bass vs her voice in the drop:** they alternate. The bass is silent under
  every command. No ducking needed.
- **Sub:** landing syllables only. Otherwise absent.
- **Lead synth vs chant:** the lead plays in the chant's rests, on the off-bars.
- **His chops:** pitched and panned inside the bass, treated as a bass layer, not
  a vocal. Do not give them vocal presence.
- **Her commands:** dry, slightly left, no reverb from the power cut onward. The
  only dry thing in a wet drop.
- **Gang chant:** dead center, stacked, wide.
- **The power cut:** a real tape stop into digital silence. Not a fade. If there
  is a reverb tail, the fakeout is gone.
- **The bass impact:** loudest transient in the file. Let it clip if it has to.
- **Pop half:** round bass, no sub, claps and guitar own the mids, the vocal sits
  on top with a slap-back. It should sound like a party, not a production.
- **The cut:** sample-accurate, mid-word, to true silence.

## Anti-corniness gate

**1. What physically happens?** A coat on since eleven, a cup sweating a ring
into a book, half a couch, a knee, a car alarm, soft ice, the lights cut, the last
train at ten to three, back stairs, a fence, a lost shoe. No abstractions anywhere
in the lyric.

**2. What is its unique danger?** Asking someone to hold still is the last calm
thing you'll say all night, and the person you said it to is the reason it ends.

**3. Which line sounds most like "a lyric"?** The original chorus tail was "the
night's got an hour or two left in it." Cut. Replaced with "Nobody's cut the lights
yet. Stay 'til somebody does." Plainer, and it plants the transition. Runner-up:
"it's warmer than it looks", kept because it is what a person says about a window.

**4. Is the central metaphor explained too completely?** "Hold still" is never
explained. "Hold on" is never explained. Nobody says the word "still" as an idea.

**5. Stock characters?** He is the smooth pop guy who never raises his voice, not
the scream guy. She is not chaos and not rescue: she has been ready to leave for
three hours and her lines in the second half are logistics. "Back stairs. Not the
front." is a plan, not a vibe.

**6. Is the genre change caused by a narrative event?** The lights get cut and the
last train is in ten minutes. The tempo perception doubles because two people are
now running. The drums double again when they go over the fence.

**7. Does the drop reuse the song's DNA?** Bass rhythm = the hook rhythm. Lead
notes = the hook notes. Chant = the hook words. Guitar riff returns at the peak.

**8. Too many importance signals?** Two chosen: the one-word chant change and the
second drum gear shift. No key change, no choir, no spoken monologue, no octave
vocal jump, no final chorus. The snare rolls and screaming lead are the genre's
vocabulary, not added emphasis.

**9. Would the chorus work with only voice and one instrument?** "Hold still, hold
still, there's room by the window" over one clean guitar is the whole intro plus
words. Yes.

**10. At least one thing the song refuses to settle?** Whether they made the
train. Whether "hold on" means wait or don't let go. Whether she had her coat on
for three hours because of the train or because of him.

**What I actually revised while drafting:**
- Cut "You've had your keys out" (keys imply a car; a car makes the train
  pointless). Coat on since eleven does the same job and gives Pre-Chorus 2 its
  argument.
- Cut every demonstrative "That's" tag before it got in. "Last one. I'm not
  joking." was drafted as "That's the last one" and rewritten.
- Deleted a planned scream from Lead B in the build. He gets shredded by the
  machine instead; his only clear line in the second half is about a shoe.
- Refused a key change at Drop 3. The gear shift in the drums does the lift.
- Refused a final melodic chorus after the drop. The brief says always building,
  so the song is not allowed to come home.

## Risks / failure modes (Suno-specific)

- **Suno smears the two halves together or resists the perceived-tempo flip.**
  Use the two-prompt Extend workflow below (Novel Idea 4).
- **Suno generates an outro after the cut.** Both the style prompt and the final
  tag say no outro. If it still tails off, trim to the last "HOLD" in post.
- **The drop plateaus after Tier 1.** The four drop tags each name what is added.
  If it still flattens, regenerate from Drop 2 with Extend.
- **The silence gets filled with a pad.** Style prompt says "two seconds of
  silence"; if Suno pads it, replace the gap with recorded room tone in post.
- **Bracket tags get sung.** All direction lives in brackets; the only
  parentheses are genuine backing vocals ("ooh", "get up", "hold on").

## Three improvements or experiments

1. Record real room tone, a real laugh and a real door for the two seconds of
   dark, and drop them into the gap in post. Generated silence is never as
   convincing as a room.
2. Try Drop 1 at 174 and 176 and keep whichever Suno grooves harder; the pop
   half's perceived tempo follows automatically.
3. Alternate take for Pre-Chorus 2: she sings the hook once, alone, over one
   guitar, before the second chorus. Rejected here because it softens the
   handover, but it might beat this version on hook score.

# Novel Ideas

Each of these is a departure from the standard safe move. The safe move is stated
so you can choose.

### Novel Idea 1 — Three gears on one grid

Standard: write the pop song at 88 and the drop at 176 and hope the generator
handles a tempo change (it usually doesn't).

Novel: the entire file is 176. The pop half is a half-time feel (perceived 88),
Drops 1–2 are drumstep (snare on 3, perceived 88 with double-speed hats), Drops
3–4 are full two-step (perceived 176). Three perceived tempos, zero actual tempo
changes. The generator never has to change its grid, which is the single most
reliable way to get an upshift out of it. It also means every layer from the pop
half is grid-compatible with the drop, which is why the intro guitar riff can
return at the peak without re-timing.

### Novel Idea 2 — The diegetic fakeout

Standard: a riser, a filter sweep, a drum fill into the drop.

Novel: the pop song is killed by its own plot. The chorus says "Nobody's cut the
lights yet. Stay 'til somebody does." Somebody does, mid-word, and the audio does
exactly what a power cut does to a party: tape stop, black, a laugh in the dark.
The listener who checks whether the track ended is having the same experience the
characters are. The transition needs no production trick because it is an event.

### Novel Idea 3 — The staircase drop

Standard: build → drop → breakdown → bigger drop → outro.

Novel: no breakdown, no second build, no outro. Four consecutive 16-bar tiers
where each adds and none subtracts, one drum gear shift in the middle, and the
song is cut mid-word at its loudest bar. Energy is monotonic from the power cut to
the end. It honours "always building" as a structural rule rather than a mood,
and it leaves the question of the train unresolved because the song never gets to
the platform.

### Novel Idea 4 — Two-prompt Extend workflow

Standard: one generation, one style prompt, hope.

Novel: if a single generation blurs the halves, generate the pop half with Prompt
A, trim at the tape stop, then use Extend with Prompt B for everything after the
silence. The lyrics box for the extension starts at the `[Break - THE LIGHTS GET
CUT ...]` tag. Prompts below.

## PRODUCTION NORTH STAR

For two minutes the listener is on a couch at a party at 2 a.m., warm, half-asleep,
listening to a man who never raises his voice ask someone to stay where she is.
She stays. The song gets sleepier, the reverb gets wider, and it ends the way warm
songs end. Then it doesn't: the power goes, the room is black, somebody laughs, and
a dry voice in the dark says get up. From that word to the last second the listener
never stops accelerating: a build that won't resolve, a drop that keeps adding, a
drum pattern that doubles under their feet, a crowd shouting his own hook at him
while he runs, and the one word in the chant changing from *still* to *on* without
anyone pointing at it. It ends on a fence, missing a shoe, mid-word, at full speed,
and you never find out about the train.
