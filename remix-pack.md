# REMIX PACK — four songs engineered for cross-genre remixing (Suno)

## The math

A remixer reuses a vocal by playing it 1:1, half-time, or double-time, then time-stretching to the target grid. Natural-sounding stretch on a sung vocal is about **±8%** (±10% with formant-preserving tools). Target ranges: house 120–128, dubstep 138–145, midtempo 98–112, drum & bass 170–176.

**No single BPM reaches all four.** Brute-forced over every half-BPM from 60–180 with factors {½, 1, 2}: the best possible single anchor is 70.5 BPM with a worst-case stretch of **20.6%** — more than double what a vocal survives. The dead zone is structural: house's ceiling (128) to DnB's floor (170) is +33%, and halving DnB lands at 85–88, which is 31% *below* house's floor. There is no bridge.

**So each song anchors a PAIR of genres exactly, and the four songs form a cycle** where every genre gets two easy remixes:

| Song | BPM | House 120–128 | Dubstep 138–145 | Midtempo 98–112 | DnB 170–176 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| COAT CHECK | **133** | **−3.8%** → 128 | **+3.8%** → 138 | — | — |
| SODIUM | **93** | — | — | **+5.4%** → 98 | **−6.5%** → 87, ×2 = 174 |
| SUBLET | **116** | **+3.4%** → 120 | — | **−3.4%** → 112 | — |
| DOWNED LINE | **158** | — | **−8.2%** → 145 | — | **+7.6%** → 170 |

Coverage: house ← COAT CHECK + SUBLET · dubstep ← COAT CHECK + DOWNED LINE · midtempo ← SODIUM + SUBLET · DnB ← SODIUM + DOWNED LINE. Worst stretch anywhere in the system: **8.2%**. COAT CHECK sits at the exact geometric midpoint of house and dubstep (√(128×138) ≈ 133), so both remixes cost the same.

## Remix-proofing rules baked into every song

1. **No tempo changes, upshifts, or rubato anywhere** — one grid start to finish, so any section's stems drop onto the remix grid.
2. **Straight sixteenths, zero swing** — swung phrasing fights every target genre's grid after stretching.
3. **Strict 8-bar sections with clean downbeat entrances** — choppable at every boundary.
4. **Both feels ship inside the song**: each track states its chorus twice — once driven, once as a half-time restatement at the same tempo — so the two remix directions are already demoed in the original.
5. **Short reverb tails that never cross the barline** — wet tails smear when stretched.
6. **Dry a cappella hook as the outro of every song** — the remixer's stem is in the master.
7. **Long-note hooks on open vowels** — held vowels survive stretching invisibly; consonant-dense runs don't. DOWNED LINE takes this to the limit: its hook is one held word.
8. **One key each, no modulation** — F minor, G minor, A minor, F♯ minor: sub roots between 41–49 Hz, and chops stay diatonic.

## 4 — DOWNED LINE
**158 BPM · F♯ minor · FEMALE-LED · bridges dubstep (−8.2%) and DnB (+7.6%)**

Danger sentence: *being dangerous is the only proof she has that the current is still on.*

Weird thing: the hook is one word held across the barline on a single open vowel — the most stretch-proof hook possible; a held vowel survives ±8% invisibly.

**Style box**

