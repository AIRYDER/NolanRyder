# SONG CONCEPT BANK

Eleven song ideas where the unusual part is the engine inside the song, not the
genre label. Everything stays in the same world: pop-punk, alt-metal, synth-pop,
garage, shoegaze, drum and bass, melodic bass, tearout, industrial.

Each card gives the subject and its danger sentence, the genre mix, the vocal
rhythm and pacing, the tonality, the one mechanism that makes the song unlike a
normal song, the payoff, and what Suno is most likely to get wrong. After the
cards there is a parts bin of loose vocal, tonal, structural, genre and subject
ideas you can mix into anything.

## At a glance

| 1 | Lot 41 | the bids are the build, the gavel is the drop | climbs the whole way |
| 2 | Swim Sideways | parallel key, three failed builds, then a drop with no build | strain, then sideways release |
| 3 | Low Battery | verses only in the gaps between chirps, and the gaps shrink | climbs, ends in silence |

### Novel Idea 1: LOT 41

- **Subject.** An estate sale. She bids against strangers for one box of her
  father's tools. The auctioneer does not know whose they were.
- **Danger.** The only way to keep it is to let a room of strangers decide what
  it is worth.
- **Genre mix.** Auctioneer-chant rap-punk over drum and bass, a gang-shout
  pop-punk hook, trap-metal weight in the low end.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Lead B is the auctioneer: spoken, rapid-fire, eighth-note
  syllables on a grid near 170 BPM, which is roughly the speed of a real auction
  chant. Lead A has no sung words at all until the end. She only shouts her
  paddle number and her bids, as hits on the offbeat.
- **Pacing.** Each lot is a short section. Lots 38 to 40 are junk and go by fast.
  Lot 41 is the box, and the bids double: 5, 10, 20, 40, 80, 160. Every bid adds
  one layer and tightens the chant.
- **Tonality.** The chant stays on one spoken pitch. Only the bass root climbs a
  step per bid, so the tension is harmonic and nobody names it.
- **Mechanism.** The bid ladder is the form. The gavel is the kick drum of the
  drop. Sanctioned weird thing: she is only numbers until the gavel.
- **Payoff.** "Sold." One gavel hit, one beat of true silence, then Lead A sings
  her first melodic line of the song, plain, nothing under it. Then the drop
  arrives, built from the chopped bid numbers.
- **Suno risk.** It will want to sing the chant. Tag it "spoken rapid-fire
  auctioneer chant, no melody." Add the gavel and the silence in post.

### Novel Idea 2: SWIM SIDEWAYS

- **Subject.** Caught in a rip current. The advice is to swim parallel to the
  shore, which means not toward it.
- **Danger.** Every instinct points at the beach, and the beach is the wrong
  direction.
- **Genre mix.** Surf-punk (tremolo-picked reverb guitar, pop-punk drums) into a
  melodic bass drop.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Lead B is the swimmer: breathless, clipped, shoving at the
  front of the beat. Lead A is the lifeguard's voice from the sand: flat, calm,
  slightly behind the beat, never raised. "Don't fight it. Parallel to the sand.
  Breathe." The calmest delivery in the song is the most intense one.
- **Pacing.** Three risers build toward the drop and each one resets to nothing.
  Those are three failed attempts to swim in. The fourth time there is no riser.
- **Tonality.** The verses sit in G minor. The escape is not a key change upward.
  It is the parallel key, G major: same root, different weather. Parallel key is
  the whole musical joke.
- **Mechanism.** The drop arrives sideways. Instead of sub weight, the energy is
  horizontal: an auto-pan swinging left and right at the tempo, the current, with
  the tremolo surf guitar playing the part a wobble bass normally would.
- **Payoff.** A drop with no build, after three builds that went nowhere. The
  stereo field collapses to mono for one beat, then opens to its full width.
- **Suno risk.** It may ignore the minor-to-parallel-major flip. Tag "key lifts
  from G minor to G major, same root note." If it will not do the sideways drop,
  settle for the major lift and add the auto-pan in post.

### Novel Idea 3: LOW BATTERY

- **Subject.** A kitchen at 3 a.m. The smoke alarm chirps every so often. Neither
  of them wants to be the one who gets the chair.
- **Danger.** Whoever fixes the small noise admits it has been going on for weeks.
- **Genre mix.** Stripped, dry pop-punk verses that mutate into drum and bass,
  where the chirp turns into the hi-hat.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Both leads sing only in the silences between chirps. When a
  chirp lands, the line stops mid-word. Lead A and Lead B take alternate gaps,
  each cut off in turn. As the gaps shrink from eight bars to four, two, one and
  then a single beat, the lines shrink with them, until the vocals are single
  syllables.
- **Tonality.** The chirp is one high ping around 3 kHz, close to a high G, so
  the song sits in G minor and the chirp is the tonic ping. Each chord makes it
  sound like a different note: the root, the fifth, the minor third.
- **Mechanism.** The chirp interval is the tempo grid. It starts as an event, then
  becomes a rhythm, then a hi-hat pattern, then a continuous tone.
- **Payoff.** The chirps run together into one sustained alarm tone, and the
  drop is a tonal bass built from that same ping. Then the battery dies and
  everything stops. Nobody ever gets the chair.
- **Suno risk.** It will not keep chirp timing. Generate the sparse half and the
  dense half as separate stretches and lay the chirp in post, tuned to G.

### Novel Idea 4: THE RING

- **Subject.** Walking home after a loud show, with the ringing in your ears.
- **Danger.** The only proof the night happened is the thing you would pay to
  make stop.
- **Genre mix.** Garage rock and pop-punk with shoegaze haze, into a melodic bass
  release.
- **Vocal rhythm.** One male lead. In the verses the vocal is low-passed and half
  heard, and lines drop out mid-syllable where his ears lose them. He is
  mishearing himself, so the lyric has to survive losing words.
- **Tonality.** A constant quiet sine tone runs under the whole song. The band
  changes chords beneath it, so the same pitch is the fifth, then the root, then
  the major seventh, then a flat ninth. One note, four moods.
- **Mechanism.** The drop is a subtraction. At the loudest point the ring cuts
  out, and a bar of the band at full volume with no ring is the most open and
  strange sound in the song.
- **Payoff.** The ring returns alone and the song ends on it.
- **Suno risk.** It tends to ignore a held tone. Add the sine in post at the
  song's key, very quiet and not too high, and bring it in during the intro.
  Keep it gentle: it is a sustained high tone.

### Novel Idea 5: TWO TOASTS, ONE MIC

- **Subject.** A wedding reception. The best man and the maid of honor used to
  date. Both toasts are to the couple and both are obviously about each other.
- **Danger.** Both speeches are true, and the room can only survive one.
- **Genre mix.** Pop-punk speech-singing over half-time drums, into melodic drum
  and bass.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Conflict duet. Both talk-sing. The drums stay half-time
  while the vocals accelerate to double and then triple speed. The vocals run
  ahead of the beat for sixteen bars and the drums have to chase. When they
  overlap they interrupt each other and finish each other's sentences wrong.
- **Pacing.** The usual order is reversed. The vocal upshift comes first and the
  drum upshift catches up.
- **Tonality.** One cheerful major key throughout, with one borrowed minor chord
  that appears only when someone nearly says the real thing.
- **Mechanism.** The drums are late to their own party. The tension is the gap
  between how fast they talk and how slow the beat is.
- **Payoff.** The DJ saves the room. A four-on-the-floor banger starts under
  them and the toasts get chopped into vocal chops: "to love... to love... to--".
  The drop is the party starting over the top of the speech.
- **Suno risk.** It will tie the vocal to the drum speed. Generate the vocals at
  double speed over an 87 BPM bed, then Extend into 174 for the drop.

### Novel Idea 6: LIGHT-LAG

- **Subject.** A crew on the long run home, and a partner on the ground. Every
  line takes a long time to arrive, and the delay shrinks as the ship comes in.
- **Danger.** You can say anything into a delay. Once it closes, you have to mean
  it live.
- **Genre mix.** Synth-pop into melodic bass.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Union duet built as a canon. Lead B sings each line. Lead A
  sings the same line back after a delay that halves each section: eight bars,
  four, two, one, half a bar, then none. The lyrics state the delay in minutes:
  "twenty-two minutes," "eleven," "four," "one."
- **Tonality.** Because the lagging line lands on a different chord than the one
  it started on, it clashes as a suspension. As the lag closes the dissonance
  resolves, so the harmony resolves because of the lag. At unison there is none.
- **Mechanism.** The form is a stretto: entries get closer together until they
  collide.
- **Payoff.** The drop is the first moment the two leads sing the same words at
  the same time. Everything before it was echo.
- **Suno risk.** Canons are hard to get in one pass. Generate Lead B's part first,
  then generate Lead A responding to it. The fallback is plain call-and-response
  with shrinking gaps.

### Novel Idea 7: QUEUE POSITION

- **Subject.** Calling the number you have been avoiding. While on hold, the
  caller rehearses the first sentence, and every draft is worse.
- **Danger.** Being put on hold is the only time you have to decide what you came
  to say.
- **Genre mix.** A bright hold-music synth loop as bait, over pop-punk, with an
  industrial-metal drop underneath.
- **Vocal rhythm.** A flat synthetic announcer counts the queue: "forty-seven,
  thirty-one, twelve, three." The numbers land on the downbeat. The rehearsed
  sentences get shorter as the number drops.
- **Pacing.** The tempo rises as the queue shrinks, so the countdown is both the
  structure and the speed.
- **Tonality.** The hold loop is a major jingle that is a hair flat against the
  band. Every time it comes back it sounds slightly sick.
- **Mechanism.** The queue number is the clock.
- **Payoff.** "A representative will be with you now." Click. The line opens and
  the other person says nothing. The drop is that silence filled with the loudest
  riff in the song. We never learn the first sentence, or who answered.
- **Suno risk.** The announcer may sing. Tag it "flat synthetic announcer, no
  melody," and record the dial-tone ending separately.

### Novel Idea 8: FINAL APPROACH

- **Subject.** A passenger is being talked down to a landing by a controller on
  the radio.
- **Danger.** The calmest voice in the song can see the same dials he can, and
  still sounds like she is reading a recipe.
- **Genre mix.** Trip-hop and industrial verses into drum and bass.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Lead A is the controller: precise, flat, almost monotone,
  checklist call-and-response. "Flaps?" "Set." Lead B is the passenger, ragged,
  speeding up. She never changes and he never calms.
- **Pacing.** Short, even radio phrases against his rising breath.
- **Tonality.** Contrary motion. The bass line descends a semitone every bar for
  twenty-four bars, the glide slope, while the lead synth, hats and his delivery
  all climb.
- **Mechanism.** Falling harmony, rising intensity. The song gets lower and
  tenser at the same time.
- **Payoff.** Touchdown is a single sub impact and then silence, with no riser
  before it. Then one calm line from her: "Tower, we're down."
- **Suno risk.** A twenty-four-bar chromatic descent will not be followed
  exactly. Tag "descending chromatic bass line" and accept an approximation.

### Novel Idea 9: HYPE MAN

- **Subject.** The guy whose only job is to yell "hey" and "let's go" is still on
  stage after the headliner has left.
- **Danger.** He built a room that sings along, and the room only knows the
  star's words.
- **Genre mix.** Pop-punk gang vocals, a drum-and-bass MC energy, a stadium synth.
- **Vocal rhythm.** The entire song is responses. He sings only what comes after
  the call: "HEY!", "WHAT!", "GO!", "LET'S GO!" The calls are a synth melody
  standing in for the absent star, so the listener hears half a conversation.
- **Pacing.** Short answers, always landing on the beat after something.
- **Tonality.** The call melody is in a big clean major key. His answers are in
  the same key but just under pitch, always a hair behind.
- **Mechanism.** The whole song is the backing-vocal slot promoted to lead.
  Sanctioned weird thing: no call is ever sung by a human until the end.
- **Payoff.** The synth call is missing, so the room goes quiet and waits. He
  sings the call himself, off-key, cracking, and for the first time the gang
  answers him.
- **Suno risk.** It will fill the calls with a lead vocal. Tag the calls as
  instrumental synth only. If it still sings, mute the lead in post.

### Novel Idea 10: OUT AND BACK

- **Subject.** A walk out of the house after a fight, and the same walk back.
- **Danger.** The same sentences sound different walking toward the door than
  walking away from it.
- **Genre mix.** Pop-punk into a melodic bass drop at the midpoint, then back out
  to pop-punk.
- **Vocal rhythm.** One lead. The second half of the song uses the first half's
  lines in reverse order, so the last line of the first verse becomes the first
  line of the last. For example, "I took my keys / I left the light on / I did
  not slam it" comes back as "I did not slam it / I left the light on / I took
  my keys," and now it reads as coming home.
- **Pacing.** Palindrome: verse, pre, chorus, then the drop at the farthest
  point, then the same sections in reverse order.
- **Tonality.** The return leg uses the same chords in reverse order. The chorus
  progression that ached on the way out resolves on the way back.
- **Mechanism.** The drop is a place, not a feeling: the farthest point from the
  house, say a gas station at 2 a.m. under a buzzing light. Everything after it
  unbuilds, removing each layer that was added.
- **Payoff.** The song ends where it started, in the same room tone, with the
  words changed only by their order.
- **Suno risk.** It cannot do a true palindrome. Write the second half's lyrics as
  the reversed lines by hand and keep the section tags mirrored.

### Novel Idea 11: STAIRWELL

- **Subject.** Fourteen floors up a stairwell because the elevator is out. The
  floor numbers are painted on the landings.
- **Danger.** From inside a stairwell you cannot tell whether you are climbing
  toward something or away from something.
- **Genre mix.** Slow emo into pop-punk into synth-pop into drum and bass.
- **Vocal rhythm.** Both leads alternate floors, each taking a landing. The
  lyrics count the floors out loud.
- **Pacing.** The tempo creeps up with every floor, from about 90 to 174 BPM.
  The genre changes because of the speed alone: the same riff at 90 is emo, at
  130 it is synth-pop, at 174 it is drum and bass.
- **Tonality.** The key rises a half step every floor. Fourteen floors is fourteen
  semitones, a little over an octave. It never resolves, because there is no
  landing to resolve onto until the roof.
- **Mechanism.** The song is one escalator. Nothing is removed and the genre is
  never changed on purpose.
- **Payoff.** The roof door. The drop is the door opening: a wall of wind noise,
  then the full drum-and-bass band over it.
- **Suno risk.** It will not do a smooth tempo ramp. Generate four segments at
  different BPMs, each a few floors, and crossfade between them. A continuous key
  climb is also unlikely, so write the half steps in the style tags per section.

# Parts bin

Loose ideas, grouped by what they change. Any of them can drop into any song.

## Vocal rhythm and pacing

- **Gap singing.** The vocal only exists in the silences. Anything that lands
  stops the line mid-word.
- **The vocal outruns the beat.** The vocals go double time while the drums stay
  half-time, and the drums catch up at the drop.
- **Breath budget.** Each line is one syllable shorter than the last. The drop is
  the first breath.
- **Countdown lyric.** Numbers are the hook, and the syllable count falls with
  them.
- **Answer-only singing.** The singer only ever responds, and the call is
  instrumental.
- **Hocket hook.** The two leads trade single notes of one melody, so the hook
  only exists when both sing and breaks the moment one stops.
- **Stretto.** A canon whose offset shrinks until the voices land together.
- **Borrowed job rhythm.** Take a speech cadence from a job: auctioneer, radio
  dispatcher, flight checklist, sports caller, safety demonstration, a hold-line
  announcer.
- **Flat voice, mutating music.** One voice stays calm and level while everything
  around it escalates.

## Tonality

- **Parallel key swap.** Minor to the major of the same root, as an escape.
- **Drone recast.** One held note, with the chords changing underneath it.
- **Contrary motion.** Bass falls while the lead and the intensity rise.
- **Mirror chorus.** The chorus plays the verse's chord progression in reverse, so
  the hook resolves where the verse ached.
- **Half-step ladder.** The key rises a semitone every section with no resolution.
- **The sick loop.** A bright sample or jingle sitting a hair flat against the
  band.
- **Meter as body.** A 7/8 verse that limps, squaring up into 4/4 at the chorus.

## Structure

- **Palindrome.** ABCD, a midpoint drop, then D'C'B'A'.
- **Anti-build.** Three builds that go nowhere, then a drop with no build at all.
- **Numeric ladder.** A bid, countdown or floor number is the form of the song.
- **Subtraction drop.** The drop is something being taken away, not added.
- **Tempo as plot.** The BPM is a thing in the story: a pulse, a queue, a chirp.

## Genre combinations

- Surf-punk with melodic bass (tremolo guitar plays the wobble).
- Auctioneer rap with drum and bass and pop-punk gang vocals.
- Shoegaze guitars over 174 breaks.
- Emo vocals in half-time over drum and bass.
- Trip-hop verses into drum and bass.
- Pop-punk with industrial found-sound percussion.
- Math-pop-punk: odd meter verses, a squared-up chorus.
- **Found sound as the bass voice.** A siren, a smoke-alarm chirp, a gavel or a
  hold-line tone is the pitch-diving tearout patch.

## Subjects (concrete, with something left unsaid)

- A smoke alarm chirping at 3 a.m.
- The hold line to the number you have been avoiding.
- A go-bag you keep calling something else.
- Holding the elevator door for someone who is not coming.
- The lost-and-found box at a venue, the morning after.
- A key that no longer fits a lock you still walk to.
- Being the designated driver at the end of the party.
- Your neighbor's schedule, known only through the wall.
- A rental car with the last renter's seat position still set.
- Backing up everything before you leave.
- A pilot light that keeps going out.

## Where I would start

1. **Lot 41.** The clearest always-building shape, an escalation with a built-in
   ending, and the auctioneer chant is something Suno can approximate.
2. **Swim Sideways.** The most unusual idea. The parallel-key joke and the drop
   with no build are the kind of thing nobody else's chorus does.
3. **Hype Man.** The most emotional payoff, with the least production risk.
