# FLOW PACK — UNDRESS mechanics × heavy bass (Suno)

Three short, fast, bright-sounding songs built on the six transferable moves from the UNDRESS hookprint, with tearout/deathstep bass carrying the drops. These are deliberately the opposite of the REBUILT pack: major-key, high hook density, dense talky flow, **all under three minutes**.

## The six moves, applied to every track

1. **Bright loop rotated to open on vi** — you enter the major-key house through the sad door. Each song uses `vi–V–I–IV` with a `V/vi` trapdoor once per chorus.
2. **One grid, two readings** — written fast, verses felt at half-time, chorus flips to the driven read with **no tempo change**. The acceleration is perceptual.
3. **Title stamped as a trochee at the loop seam** — TOOTH-ache, MARK-down, HOUSE mo-ney. Word-reset and harmony-reset land together.
4. **Compulsive count** — the confession phrase repeats an odd, slightly-too-many number of times ("check it" ×23, "what's the catch" ×22, "still up" ×19 across each song). Form enacts content.
5. **Friction pair as signature** — sugary bounce × unwell confession, named per song.
6. **Hook inside 45 seconds, song under 3:00.**

## What makes the flow the flow

The verses are **rap-adjacent but sung**: 3–5 syllable bursts, run-on sentences that spill across the barline, dense internal rhyme, direct address, imperatives, self-interrupting lists. Narrow melodic range — almost one note — so the chorus leaping a fifth feels enormous. The verses should sound like someone talking too fast about something they've decided is fine.

**Heavy bass placement:** the drop lands *after* the chorus, not instead of it, and the bass speaks the hook's syllabic rhythm. Guitars and bass alternate — never stacked.

## 3 — HOUSE MONEY

Danger sentence: *they're both up, so neither of them has to be the one who says stop.*
Friction pair: jackpot-chime euphoria × two people watching a life get spent and agreeing not to mention it.
Loop: Bm–A–D–G (vi–V–I–IV), one F♯ trapdoor per chorus. Compulsive count: "still up" ×6.
Weird thing: they never once disagree — the whole song is two people granting each other permission, and the danger is entirely in what neither says.
