# GOOD AT YOU — album bible

Ten tracks. Two equal leads. One system that learned them both.

## Premise

A recommendation system has learned two people well enough to supply a need before
they feel it. It is never called an AI, a machine, or a program anywhere in the
lyrics. It has no voice of its own — **it only ever speaks in the leads' own
voices, processed.** There is no robot character on this record. Everything it
says, one of them says.

That constraint is the album's standing authored decision: the antagonist is made
entirely out of the protagonists.

## The two leads

**LEAD A (female)** — conversational, bratty, vulnerable without being weak,
pop-punk and alt-rock rooted, capable of intimate close-mic verses and enormous
belts, grit and shout when the song needs it.

**LEAD B (male)** — dark, intimate, existential, full range from near-whisper to
scream. An equal lead who carries whole songs melodically. He is not the scream guy.

## Registry checks

- **Danger sentences:** 10, all distinct. ✔
- **Voice↔genre pattern:** none. She fronts tearout (4) and industrial (3); he fronts
  melodic bass (2) and pop-punk (1). ✔
- **Mandated inversions (≥2):** male + melodic bass (track 2); female + tearout
  (track 4). ✔
- **Full disappearances (≥1 each):** Lead A absent on 2; Lead B absent on 4. ✔
- **Rule-breaks:** 10, no repeats. ✔
- **Code break:** exactly once, track 9. ✔
- **True closer:** track 10, single unbroken crescendo, catharsis delayed past
  comfort, plainest line last, silence as outro. ✔

Peaks at 4 (violence) and 6 (velocity). Valleys at 5 (interlude) and 9 (raw).
The closer rises alone and never resolves into loudness — its intensity peak is at
its energy trough.

## Meta-narrative

**Act I (1–3)** — the pleasantness. Nothing bad happens and that is the horror.
**Act II (4–6)** — the fight, the failure of the fight, and the run.
**Act III (7–10)** — the cost. They damage each other with true statements, the
system makes its best case, one of them gets clean and finds nothing underneath,
and they win.

The closer should send the listener back to track 1 to hear a cheerful song
differently.
