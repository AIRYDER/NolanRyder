## The unified diagnosis

The three judges worked blind to each other and found the same fault from three
angles: **the album's house move is withholding, and it never stops.**

- *Composition judge:* "Restraint has hardened into the formula. Every track's
  sanctioned weird thing is a subtraction — mute the guitars, deny the payoff,
  remove the drums, remove the band, remove the hiss. Withholding is only
  powerful against an expectation, and by track 9 the album has trained the
  listener to expect withholding, so the last three refusals cost nothing."
- *A&R judge:* every singalong mechanism was deliberately deleted — gang vocals
  absent from six tracks, choruses written quieter than verses, two tracks with
  no chorus, the leads forbidden to sing together on track 7.
- *Lyric judge:* the compensating tic — **the demonstrative summary tag.** A scene
  lands, then a short sentence beginning with *That's* arrives to gloss it.
  "That was the point." / "That's the whole file." / "That's the entire offer." /
  "That's not luck, that's homework." / "That's the whole night, I let it."
  This is the benchmarks' thesis-stating sin smuggled into a plain register.

This traces back to the album bible, not to the writers. Ten rule-breaks were
specified and every one of them was a removal.

## Required fixes

1. **One climax must arrive full, on time, and unironically loud.** Track 6 (the
   DnB upshift, still being written) is the natural home — it is the only track
   whose rule-break is not a subtraction. If 6 does not do this, promote track 4.
2. **Delete the *That's* tag in at least half its appearances.** The record
   already knows how: "I always make too much" carries no gloss and is the best
   moment on the album.
3. **Make track 4 chantable.** It has the right shape (title-as-hook, gang
   answer) but every line is angry. Needs a two-note melodic lift on the answer,
   the hook 20–30s earlier, and one bratty non-grim line — the equivalent of
   "So what! I like the hurt."
4. **Break the drop template.** Lullaby cell + bVI→i + supersaw generates the
   melodic half of tracks 2, 7 and 8 — they are mutually swappable, which
   destroys track 7's argument that its two halves are two people.
5. **Resequence 7 and 8.** Both peak at 9–10, both half-time, both pair a
   supersaw passage with a mechanical one. They blur. Put 9 or 6 between them.
6. **Vary the endings.** Six of nine tracks end on refrigerator hum, a chime, or
   a compressor cycling. By track 8 it is predictable, which drains the closer.

## Per-track headline notes

- **08 GOOD AT YOU** — best on the record. The hook melody, the album's four-note
  DNA and the title are one object. Drop 2 changes only timbre and reverb: same
  chords, inversions, octave, rhythm. The mutation costs zero notes. Fix: "That's
  not luck, that's homework" is copywriting in the album's most-repeated line.
- **09 UNDER IT** — best-written. "Put the plate in the machine. It takes eight
  seconds." Instruments leave in the order his story falls apart. Code break
  lands: "It was an app on my phone. I deleted it in April."
- **04 IT'S NOT IN THE PHONE** — most commercial, weakest lyric. "NOT IN THE
  GLASS, NOT IN THE BATTERY" is negation inventory; battery is there for syllables.
- **10 NONE LEFT** — highest composition score (9.0), lowest listenability outside
  the interlude. Energy 9→4 against intensity 9→10 is the album's only fully
  engineered divergence. The unfilled three-bar hole where his voice was is the
  best-placed silence on the record.
- **02 THE WARM** — the drop is verse 1's vocal melody with his breaths preserved
  as rests. Delete the verse and the drop has no lead line. Unforgeable.
- **05 interlude** — carries five DNA elements with nobody playing an instrument,
  and hands the unresolved cadence across the track boundary to 6. Scores 1.0 on
  hook, correctly and by design.
