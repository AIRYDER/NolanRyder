## The unified diagnosis

Working blind, the three judges found one fault from three angles: **the album's
house move is withholding, and it almost never stops.**

- *Composition:* "Restraint has hardened into the formula. Every track's
  sanctioned weird thing is a subtraction. Withholding is only powerful against an
  expectation, and by track 9 the album has trained the listener to expect
  withholding, so the last three refusals cost nothing."
- *A&R:* every singalong mechanism was deleted on purpose — gang vocals absent
  from six tracks, choruses quieter than verses, two tracks with no chorus, the
  leads forbidden to sing together on 7.
- *Lyric:* the compensating tic — **the demonstrative summary tag**, present on
  **ten of ten tracks**. A scene lands, then a sentence beginning with *That's*
  arrives to gloss it. "That's the whole file." / "That's the entire offer." /
  "That's not luck, that's homework." / "That's the whole night, I let it."

This traces to the album bible, not the writers: ten rule-breaks were specified
and every one was a removal.

**Track 6 is the exception and the cure.** Its Drop 1 is energy 10 against
intensity 8 — the only point on the record where energy exceeds intensity. That
single inversion is what makes it read as payment rather than restraint, and it is
why the composition judge passed the album's "one climax must arrive full, on
time and unironically loud" requirement.

## The fix list, in priority order

1. **Eight bars of unison in track 6, Drop 1 only.** A&R: "You are one 8-bar
   unison chorus away from a genuine anthem and you've banned it on principle."
   The rule-break (leads never resolve into the same melody) should be scoped to
   *after the divergence* — at Drop 1 they are still united, so it costs nothing
   narratively and buys the album its anthem. Lock "One more flight and we're out"
   to the grid at 174 for eight bars.
2. **Push "say when, say when."** Two syllables, repeated, answerable — the actual
   mass singalong on the record, currently under-used.
3. **Delete the *That's* tag in at least half its appearances.** Cheapest in the
   verses (06, 03), most expensive in track 8's hook and track 10's last line.
   The model for keeping one: "that's the ramp, that's the ramp" survives because
   she is pointing at an actual ramp at speed — deictic, not glossing.
4. **Make track 4 chantable.** Right shape, wrong temperature: every line is
   angry. Needs a two-note melodic lift on the answer, the hook 20–30s earlier,
   and one bratty non-grim line.
5. **Break the drop template.** Cell + bVI→i → drop lead now generates five drops
   (2, 6, 7-his-half, 8-Drop-1, and the closer's climax). Track 6's Reese extends
   the procedure rather than breaking it. Differentiate at least one by rhythm or
   register, or track 7 loses its argument that its two halves are two people.
6. **Resequence.** 6, 7 and 8 are now three consecutive peaks, and 7 and 8 blur —
   both half-time, both pairing a supersaw passage with a mechanical one. 5→6 is
   the album's best adjacency; move 7 or 8, not 6.
7. **Vary the endings.** Six of ten tracks end on refrigerator hum, a chime or a
   compressor cycling. By track 8 it is predictable, which drains the closer.

## Corrections to earlier claims

- **Track 6's forwards/backwards cell is not perceptible as retrograde.** Nobody
  hears a four-note figure as reversed. What is audible is the consequence: she
  lands on the tonic, he hangs unresolved on the 5th, every time, in both voices
  and both basslines. Produce and describe it as **landing-note opposition**.
- **The two-melodies-always rule has a real cost.** A crowd has no single melody
  to learn and will shout in whichever tune it grabbed first. Fix #1 addresses it.

## Singles

1. **RUN THE SAME ROOM** — the front door. The only straightforwardly fun track.
2. **IT'S NOT IN THE PHONE** — the shout.
3. **GOOD AT YOU** — the title track and the emotional single.

## Honest verdict

Better songs: **yes, decisively, on writing and composition** — +2.2 and +2.65
over the benchmarks, with every one of ten tracks clearing the bar.

Catchier songs: **no.** 5.20 vs 6.5. Six of ten sit at or below the benchmark on
hook. Before track 6 this was an admired album with no single; with it, the record
has exactly one song that beats both benchmarks outright and can carry it.

### Detail

**Track 6 — the unison.** Drop 1 now opens with eight bars of both leads locked to
the 174 grid, same words, same melody, gang-stacked. The rule-break is rescoped
from "never the same melody" to **"never the same melody again after the
divergence."** This is not a concession: they share exactly one unison in their
lives, it happens before the exit goes past, and it becomes the thing the song
takes away. It also gives them the only moment either travels at the speed of the
music — and they only manage it together. Style prompt re-verified at 994 chars.

**Track 4 — chantability.** The gang answer no longer repeats her flatly; it lifts
two notes (root → b3), the only melodic movement in the track, which is what makes
it learnable in one pass. "NOT IN THE GLASS, NOT IN THE BATTERY" (abstract
negation inventory, battery present for syllables) became **"NOT UNDER MY HEEL,
NOT IN THE SINK"** and **"NOT IN THE BIN, NOT IN THE WATER"** — only things she
actually did to the object. Added the bratty non-grim line the judge asked for:
**"SO WHAT — I'LL BUY ANOTHER ONE."** Shrugged, funny, and the most hopeless line
in the song.

**Track 8 — the hook.** "That's not luck, that's homework" (copywriting, in the
album's most-repeated position) became **"Four years of you. / I got good at you."**
A receipt instead of a boast — and it now states the motif three times per chorus
instead of twice, so the fix helps the hook score as well as the lyric score.

**Kept deliberately:** "that's the ramp, that's the ramp" (06 — she is pointing at
an actual ramp at speed), "Nobody moved it. That's just where they go." (10 —
about mugs, physical), "That's not — I know you're there." (10 — interrupted
speech, not a gloss), "and I wanted it. That's the part I'd like back." (07 — a
confession, not an explanation).
